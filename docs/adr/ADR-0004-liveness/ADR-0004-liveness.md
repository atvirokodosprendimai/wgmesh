## Decisions of M

- **M1. A successful mesh probe counts as alive evidence next to the handshake** (M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"). A pair that relays through an introducer never has a handshake with each other (traffic is encrypted to the relay's key), so a handshake-only rule would leave the pairs of #850 impact 1 cycling five minutes up and five down. Rejected: handshake only; also counting received exchange datagrams (that is control plane, which `LastSeen` already is).
- **M2. A relayed peer stays configured with its endpoint and no AllowedIPs** (M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"), which is the fix #852 proposes. Cost accepted: the 25-second persistent keepalive (`pkg/wireguard/apply.go:157`) and handshake attempts go to every relayed peer all the time. Rejected: a probe window (configure it for 20 seconds every five minutes); leaving the one-way door.
- **M3. The daemon learns of a wake from a wall-clock gap watcher** (M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"): a 2-second ticker, a gap of more than 10 seconds means "resumed", pure Go. Release builds are `CGO_ENABLED=0` (`.goreleaser.yml:14`); OS power notifications (`IORegisterForSystemPower`, `NSWorkspace`) need cgo or a helper process and are deferred in `docs/adr/BACKLOG.md`. The watcher is a heuristic: it also fires on a long stop-the-world pause or a clock step, which is harmless here. Not measured on a Mac.
- **M4. Staging is the two-release rule with two switches** (M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"): `node.EnforceLiveness` (N+1: the evidence counts, restored entries get their grace, the wake grace applies) and `daemon.EnforceRelayProbe` (N+2: relayed peers stay configured), both `false` at N; at N the verdict is computed beside the legacy one and every disagreement is counted. Rejected: everything acting at N; staging only #852.
- **M5. After a wake, the first STUN observation replaces the local endpoint at once** (M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"). ADR-0002 rule R7 (T9) accepts a STUN-only change after three equal observations, three minutes at the 60-second refresh, which would delay the recovery #829 asks for; the resume trigger passes a `stun-resume` source (`daemon.EndpointSTUNResume`) that `ObserveEndpoint` applies at once. **Confirmed by M 2026-11-15 22:46** (the ADR-0002 R7 row, which `docs/admit-rollout.md:24` and `docs/adr/BACKLOG.md:19` repeat): it adds one case to the accepted ADR-0002 R7, and T5 waits for nothing but T4.
- **M6. The wake grace is 60 seconds** (`ResumeGrace`, M 2026-11-15: "Pagal visas rekomendacijas"), the time for one STUN refresh, one announce and one WireGuard rekey; not measured. Rejected: `HandshakeStaleAfter` (150 seconds), which would hide a peer that really died while we slept.

## Context

**The pipe.** A peer's liveness today is `evidence -> a threshold at the place that happens to read it`: the PeerStore keeps one timestamp (`LastSeen`) that only the peer's own announcements refresh, and every other reader brings its own thresholds and its own evidence. In ETL terms the extract step has four sources (control-plane packets, WireGuard handshakes, mesh probes, the cache), the load step is whatever each reader does (hide, relay, evict, restore), and the transform between them does not exist as one step. The missing step is **assess**: given the evidence a node has about one peer (when it last spoke, when WireGuard last handshook, when a probe last returned, when it was restored, whether the machine just woke), say `Alive`, `Suspect` or `Dead`, and let every reader ask that one function.

**What the four issues are, checked against the code** (read at `b8b83f3`, every line number below re-read at `fdd5af2`; nothing here was run on a live mesh, the issues' own reproductions were scratch tests that are not in the repository):

The reconcile loop does not call a remove: `applyDesiredPeerConfigs` removes every WireGuard peer that is not in the desired set (`daemon.go:910-965`, the removal at `:914`), and the desired set is built from `GetActive` (`reconcile`, `daemon.go:670`, `:673`). Handshakes are read in the daemon (`GetLatestHandshakes`, `pkg/wireguard/apply.go:200`; in `buildDesiredPeerConfigs`, `daemon.go:703`, not in `reconcile`) and used to choose a path, to skip probes and to evict, and never written to the store.

- *#829 is real as stated, and has a second cause the issue does not name.* There is no power or wake handling (`grep -rnE "wake|power|NSWorkspace|IORegister|SIGCONT" --include=*.go . | grep -v _test.go | grep -v "time.Sleep"` lists only `pkg/nurture/sender.go` text, 2026-11-15). Beyond slow recovery, a wake mixes two clock domains: `LastSeen` is stored with `time.Now()` and so carries a monotonic reading, and the stdlib ages it by the monotonic clock, which does not run while the machine is suspended (documented for `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` on Linux; not measured on macOS); a handshake time is `time.Unix(ts, 0)` and is aged by the wall clock, which does. After a long sleep the control plane looks fresh and every handshake looks hours old, so `shouldTreatPeerAsStale` (`daemon.go:1480-1485`) sees a stale handshake with no transfer growth for every peer, attempts a reconnect on the first health cycle and evicts on the second (`checkPeerHealth`, `:1421-1478`, the calls at `:1462` and `:1466`; 40 seconds with `HealthCheckInterval` 20 seconds). Each eviction also leaves a 5-minute tombstone (ADR-0002 T3, merged), so after a wake the node would refuse its own peers' second-hand mentions for five minutes: the wake grace below is what keeps ADR-0002's tombstone from doing damage here.

## Decision

1. **One pure judgement, `node.Assess(Evidence, now) Liveness`, and `PeerInfo.Alive(now)` over it, in `pkg/node/liveness.go`.** `Liveness` has the constants `Alive`, `Suspect`, `Dead` (the admission outcome `node.Verdict` already exists). `Evidence` is four times, any of which may be zero: `LastSeen` (the peer's own packet), `LastDataPlane` (the newest of its last WireGuard handshake and its last successful mesh probe), `RestoredAt` (when the cache restored it) and, store-wide, `ResumedAt` (when the node last woke). The rule is one line: take the newest non-zero time, `ref`; `age = now - ref`, all of them as wall-clock instants with the monotonic reading stripped (`Round(0)`), which removes the mixed-clock defect of #829; `age < PeerDeadTimeout` is `Alive`, `age <= PeerRemoveTimeout` is `Suspect`, older is `Dead`. No new timeout is added to the aliveness rule: a handshake is renewed about every two minutes while two peers can talk (WireGuard rekeys on traffic; the keepalive at `apply.go:157` is traffic), so five minutes after the last one the peer is as silent as a peer whose announcements stopped five minutes ago. `GetActive` returns the `Alive` peers (as today, with the new meaning), `GetProbeable` returns `Alive` and `Suspect` peers (what the probe can reach of them is stated under Existing Primitives), `CleanupStale` removes `Dead` ones, `IsDead` is `Dead` or unknown. `PeerInfo.LastAlive(now)` is the same newest-of rule returned as a time, for callers that keep a window of their own: the 90-second, 45-second and 2-minute tests of the class audit are windows, not verdicts: each reader keeps its own window and operator and takes the age from one `PeerInfo` helper that gives the legacy `LastSeen` age while `EnforceLiveness` is false and the `LastAlive` age when it is true (Decision 7). `Get`, `GetAll`, `GetActive` and `GetProbeable` return copies, so every copy carries the store-wide `resumedAt` stamped into it (an unexported field) and `PeerInfo.Alive(now)` outside the store sees a wake. The store reads its clock through `ps.now()` in `GetActive`, `IsDead` and the merge as it already does in `CleanupStale`. The function is pure: it reads no clock except through `now` and no store.
2. **Evidence is written by the node that measured it, never by a claim.** `PeerStore.ObserveDataPlane(key, at)` sets `LastDataPlane` (monotonic, never moves backwards, no event, no change to any claim field; it takes `ps.mu.Lock` like `SetLatency`, `store.go:400`); `reconcile` reads the handshake map once at its top and calls it for every peer with a non-zero handshake *before* it calls `GetActive`, and `probePeer` calls it on success. It is a local measurement, like `SetLatency`, so it does not go through ADR-0002's `Offer` and the guard of `TestNoDirectPeerStoreMutation` does not cover it (that guard names `Update`, `SetPeerDirectly`, `Remove`). A restore sets `RestoredAt = ps.now()` when the store merges a `Restored` candidate for a key it does not hold (T3); the entry is then `Alive` for `PeerDeadTimeout` and `Suspect` until `PeerRemoveTimeout` if nothing else is heard, and `HeardFirstHand` stays false, so `CleanupStale` leaves no tombstone for it (it tombstones only first-hand peers, `store.go:383`). `SavePeerCache` writes `LastContact` (the newest of `LastSeen` and `LastDataPlane`: measured contact only, never the restore or wake grace, so a restart or a wake cannot renew the 24-hour cache expiry of a peer nobody has heard) so that a node that was connected by handshakes saves a fresh time.
3. **Wake grace.** `pkg/daemon/resume.go` holds `DetectResume(prev, now time.Time, threshold time.Duration) bool` (pure: the wall-clock gap, monotonic stripped, is above the threshold) and a loop, started in both run paths (`Run`, `daemon.go:349-431`, and `RunWithDHTDiscovery`, `:1662-1782`), that ticks every `ResumeCheckInterval` (2 seconds) and, on a gap of more than `ResumeGapThreshold` (10 seconds), calls `PeerStore.NoteResume(now)`, signals the reconcile loop and calls the resume hook. `ResumedAt` is evidence for every peer, so all peers stay `Alive` for `PeerDeadTimeout` after a wake; health reconnect and eviction (`attemptPeerReconnect` `daemon.go:1462` and `evictPeerFromPool` `:1466`, both in `checkPeerHealth`), probe eviction (`:1251`) and the stale-handshake branch of the relay choice (`shouldRelayPeerWithSubnets`, `:819-840`) are skipped for `ResumeGrace` (60 seconds) after `ResumedAt`, because handshake ages are meaningless until WireGuard has had one rekey. Constants not measured; M6.
4. **Resume actions** (the part of #829 that is speed). On a resume the daemon runs the same functions the periodic loops run, now: it signals the reconcile loop (a buffered channel; `reconcile()` is never called from the resume goroutine, because the apply step is not serialised and would run beside `reconcileLoop`), and calls `OnResume()` on the one discovery layer it holds (`d.dhtDiscovery`, `daemon.go:78`) when that implements `node.Resumer`; LAN and gossip live inside `DHTDiscovery` and are driven by its `OnResume`. The DHT layer implements it (T5): one STUN refresh through `LocalNode.ObserveEndpoint(daemon.EndpointSTUNResume, ...)` (ADR-0002 T9, applied at once; M5), one announce (`announce()` to the infohash and `SendAnnounce` to every active peer's mesh IP) and a reset of the rendezvous back-off so the next cycle may punch. These actions are additive and local, and act from N; only the grace in 3 waits for the switch.
5. **Eviction goes through ADR-0002's `Withdraw`.** This record adds no code that removes a peer: `CleanupStale` (which tombstones first-hand peers, ADR-0002 T3), `evictPeerFromPool` (`Withdraw(key, "evicted")`) and GOODBYE are the three removers. `TombstoneTTL` (5 minutes) is not touched. Because ADR-0002 keeps the tombstone in memory, nothing here persists one; #851's restore is not affected by a tombstone, since tombstones do not survive the restart that precedes a restore (and `Decide` refuses a tombstoned key for any hand but first-hand, `pkg/node/admit.go:105`).
6. **Relay return keeps `dht-transitive` readable and adds the signal that was missing.** The consumer at `daemon.go:857-858` stays exactly as it is: it is the prior for a peer for which there is no handshake entry. What was missing is the entry: a relayed peer is removed from the interface, so no handshake can ever be recorded. T6 keeps a relayed peer configured with its endpoint and **no AllowedIPs** (`desiredPeerConfig.probeOnly`), so a data packet from it direct is dropped by WireGuard (the relay path stays the only data path) while the persistent keepalive keeps trying a handshake; a fresh handshake is then seen by the check at `:819-840` (`return false` when `< HandshakeStaleAfter`, `:822`) *before* the `dht-transitive` prior is consulted, and the hysteresis of 3 cycles returns the peer to direct. The replacement signal for the label's prior is therefore the observed handshake, and the label is not removed (ADR-0002 Decision 9). A relayed peer that never handshakes stays relayed, which is correct. **A hazard this creates:** a relayed peer today has handshake time 0 and so is never stale (`shouldTreatPeerAsStale`, `:1481`); a probe-only peer will have a handshake time, and when it goes stale `checkPeerHealth` would call `attemptPeerReconnect` (`:1510-1531`), which sets the peer with its full allowed IPs (`reconnectAllowedCSV`, `:1493`) and takes the mesh IP from the relay. T6 therefore makes `checkPeerHealth` and `attemptPeerReconnect` skip relay-routed (`isRelayRoutedPeer`, `:1598`) and probe-only peers.
7. **Staging, the two-release rule.** Two switches, both false at N, both `atomic.Bool` (they are read by many goroutines; tests call `.Store(true)`). `node.EnforceLiveness` (in `pkg/node`): at N `GetActive`, `CleanupStale` and the four outside readers compute the new verdict beside the legacy age test and count every case where the new verdict would have kept a peer that the legacy test drops (`wgmesh_liveness_disagree_total{site}`), while the legacy test acts; the count goes through a per-store hook, `PeerStore.SetLivenessDisagreeHook`, wired in `NewDaemon` the way `Policy.OnWarn` is wired to `RecordAdmitWarning` (`admit.go:83`, `daemon.go:294-307`, `metrics.go:160`), not through a package-level setter that parallel tests would race on; at N+1 the verdict acts, with the wake grace and the restore grace. `daemon.EnforceRelayProbe`: at N the daemon exports the age of every relay route (`wgmesh_relay_route_age_seconds`, the evidence that the one-way door exists in the field); at N+2 the probe-only peers are configured. The resume actions of Decision 4 and the evidence writes of Decision 2 act from N, and so does the probe loop's visit of every `GetProbeable` peer (they add packets and a field; they refuse nothing, and the probe visits more peers, never fewer). **What the wire looks like:** no byte changes. A relayed peer receives handshake attempts from us from N+2, which an older node answers like any other.

What would make this decision fail: (a) a reader that compares `LastSeen` itself, caught by `TestNoDirectLastSeenAge`; (b) a clock mix that makes a peer fresh in one place and stale in another, caught by `TestAssessIgnoresMonotonicReading`; (c) a wake that evicts, caught by `TestResumeGraceBlocksHealthEviction`; (d) a probe-only peer that carries traffic, caught by the apply test of T6 (an empty AllowedIPs list is what is sent). Only (a) is red once its test exists; the others exist once their task's first step has run.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Refresh `LastSeen` from the handshake in the health loop** (the issue's suggested fix). Rejected: `LastSeen` is what ADR-0002 reads for `HeardFirstHand` and the merge rules, and a handshake is not the peer describing itself; the next source (a probe, a restore) would be one more writer of the same field with its own meaning. A separate evidence time and one reader keeps `LastSeen` honest and makes the question "who says it is alive" greppable.
- **Add a "cached" state beside active** (the #851 suggestion). Rejected: it is `Suspect` with a reason, and the consumers (exchange, DHT, reconcile) would each need to learn the new state; `RestoredAt` as evidence reuses `Assess` and changes no consumer.
- **Leave the thresholds per reader and only add the missing evidence to each.** Rejected: seven age tests and four handshake tests already disagree (2 minutes at `dht.go:872`, `:1270`, `:1281`; 90 seconds at `daemon.go:722`; 150 seconds at `daemon.go:1484`; 5 and 10 minutes in the store); a new writer would have to be added to each of them.
- **Hide the relay problem by removing the `dht-transitive` consumer** (`daemon.go:857-858`). Rejected: M decided on 2026-11-15 (ADR-0002 Decision 9) that the label stays because that code reads it; here it stays and a handshake overrides it.
- **OS power notifications first.** Not rejected, deferred by M3: exact, but needs cgo or a helper in a `CGO_ENABLED=0` release.
- **Raise `PeerDeadTimeout`.** Rejected: it hides #850 for longer and slows every real removal, and it does nothing for the wake case.

## Implementation

See `tasks/README.md`. Seven tasks in five waves (the tasks share `daemon.go` and `store.go` and run one after another on one branch): T1 the pure `Assess` and the store reading it, T2 the evidence writers and every reader, plus the guard test (#850), T3 restore (#851), T4 wake detection and grace (#829), T5 the DHT layer's resume actions (#829), T6 relay return (#852), T7 specs, release notes and backlog rows.

**Ordering against ADR-0002 (merged: `origin/main` `f3860c6`).** Every prerequisite on ADR-0002 (T1 to T3 for the store and the `Restored` merge, T9 for `ObserveEndpoint`) is on main; this record is built on that tree. The tasks still share files and run one after another on one branch: T1 and T2 edit `pkg/node/store.go` and `daemon.go`, T3 edits `cache.go` and the `Restored` merge, T5 edits `dht.go` and `daemon.go` (the `EndpointSource` constants and `NATType`), T6 edits `applyDesiredPeerConfigs` and `wireguard.SetPeer`. **The first line of each task's commit message names the base commit, `f3860c6`, or the later main commit it was rebuilt on.** If a later change renames a name used here (`Withdraw`, `Hand`, `Restored`, `ObserveEndpoint`), the task follows the code.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** a peer that WireGuard or the probe can reach is not dropped because a DHT or exchange outage silenced its announcements; a relay-only pair stops cycling five minutes up and five down; a restart after more than five minutes reconnects from the cache for five minutes; a wake does not evict and tombstone every peer, and the first STUN refresh and announce happen seconds after the wake; a relayed peer can return to the direct path; one function answers "alive", so the next evidence source is one more field and one more line in `Assess`.
- **Negative:** until N+1 nothing changes for the operator except counters. A peer whose control plane is dead but whose tunnel works stays in `GetActive` and is advertised in `KnownPeers` and gossip (it is alive; M1 decided that the probe counts). Probe-only peers cost a keepalive per relayed peer every 25 seconds from N+2. The wake detector can fire on a long pause or a clock step; the consequence is one extra announce and a 60-second grace.
- **Neutral:** `LastSeen` keeps its meaning and its writers; the six `wg show` forks stay six; `IsDead` stays unused.

## Out of Scope

- One `wg show` per tick shared by every reader (permanent: boundary: a performance change, not a liveness decision; the readers' answers do not change). 
- OS power-event integration through cgo or a helper process (deferred: docs/adr/BACKLOG.md).
- Per-peer adaptive timeouts or a failure detector with a score (permanent: boundary: one threshold per class keeps `Assess` a one-line rule; revisit only with measured flapping).
- The tombstone lifetime, `Withdraw`, and whether a claim is admitted (permanent: boundary: ADR-0002 owns them; this record calls `Withdraw` and never decides admission).
- The local endpoint rule and the multi-candidate endpoint (permanent: boundary: ADR-0002 T9 owns `ObserveEndpoint`; this record adds one source label to it, M5, confirmed by M 2026-11-15 22:46).
- Which relay an introducer picks and the relay selection hash (`selectRelayForPeer`, `daemon.go:868`) (permanent: boundary: the relay choice is not the liveness question).
- The apply step as a whole (diff of desired and actual WireGuard state, routes, iptables), including #867 and #872 (permanent: boundary: owned by the apply-step group; T6 adds one state, "peer present, no AllowedIPs", to the desired set and nothing else).
- Windows power events (permanent: fact: releases are built for linux and darwin only; citation: file `.goreleaser.yml:15`).

## Rollback

N only adds fields, a function, a hook and metrics, and runs the legacy test beside the new one; reverting N removes them and changes no persisted data except that the cache file's `last_seen` may hold a `LastContact`, which an older version reads as a `LastSeen` (it is a valid time; the entry may look fresher than before and the older version ages it out as it always did). N+1 is returned to warn mode by `node.EnforceLiveness.Store(false)`. N+2: `daemon.EnforceRelayProbe.Store(false)` makes the next reconcile remove the probe-only peers again (`applyDesiredPeerConfigs` removes what is not desired, `daemon.go:914`); the WireGuard interface returns to today's state without a restart. Nothing is written to disk by the resume loop.
